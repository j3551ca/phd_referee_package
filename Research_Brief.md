# Jessica M. Caleta — PhD research brief

Autumn 2027 entry

## Research direction

My primary interests are computational molecular epidemiology, pathogen evolution, and genomic surveillance. I want to investigate how pathogens emerge and spread across human, animal, and environmental systems, and develop reproducible computational methods that translate sequence data into useful public-health evidence. I am also interested in computational biotechnology and broader biomedical applications of genomics.

## Preparation

I hold an MSc in Microbiology and Immunology and a BSc with Distinction in Microbiology and Oceanography from the University of British Columbia. My research spans viral intrahost diversity, Bayesian phylogenetics, wastewater surveillance, antimicrobial resistance, and public-health bioinformatics. I work with Python, R, Bash, Nextflow, and high-performance computing, and have coauthored nine journal publications.

## Selected contributions

- **Wastewater genomic surveillance:** Developed and validated [WasteFlow](https://github.com/BCCDC-PHL/WasteFlow) to support surveillance across 15 sites and five BC Health Authorities, with regular reporting to public-health partners. Subsequently statistically analyzed sequencing cadence to reduce annual wastewater WGS costs by 50%.
- **Pathogen evolution and outbreak analysis:** Contributed Bayesian evolutionary analysis of Canada's first domestic human H5N1 case within two days, supporting collaborative publications in [Emerging Infectious Diseases](https://doi.org/10.3201/eid3106.241862) and [The New England Journal of Medicine](https://doi.org/10.1056/nejmc2415890). These were team investigations, with my contribution centered on computational analysis.
- **Reproducible computational methods:** Built [riboscope](https://github.com/j3551ca/riboscope) for resistance-mutation detection in *Treponema pallidum* and migrated routine phylogenomic workflows to SLURM-enabled Nextflow pipelines, including [covflo](https://github.com/BCCDC-PHL/covflo) and [fluflo](https://github.com/BCCDC-PHL/fluflo). Accelerated more than 100 routine phylogenomic analyses threefold through workflow and data-management improvements.
- **Research communication and teaching:** Co-developed and taught a graduate Advanced Bioinformatics course with Tara Newman at the University of Lethbridge, delivering 12 lectures and supervising student projects. Presented wastewater surveillance work to PHAC and at the National GLTO Summit.

My current Research Affiliate role in Dr. Brenda Rubenstein's laboratory at Brown University, begun in October 2026, extends my computational work to protein–protein interaction networks across cancer subtypes.
